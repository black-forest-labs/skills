# Review scorecard

Judge the full clip at speed, then frame by frame around the event. A request that returned successfully is not the same as a shot worth using.

## Drafts and full renders are judged differently

A draft is a low-step preview. It will look rougher than the final: less fine material detail, softer texture, coarser grain. That is the trade it makes for speed and cost.

- **On a draft**, judge sections 1–4 only: editorial utility, physical legibility, composition and camera, continuity. These carry through to the full render, because `draft_enhance` replays the same prompt, seed, and settings.
- **Defer sections 5 and 6** — material and light, audio finish — until the full-quality render. A draft cannot answer them fairly.

Rejecting a draft for softness discards a concept that would have worked. Rejecting it for a broken event, an unreadable frame, or a continuity jump is correct — those do not improve with more steps.

## 1. Editorial utility

- Does the clip work as B-roll, a bridge, a hero texture, or a clean capability insert?
- Is there a clear setup, event, and payoff?
- Is the concept interesting, not merely correct or attractive?
- Is there a usable opening or ending frame?

## 2. Physical legibility

- Is the cause visible?
- Does contact occur in the right place?
- Does the response travel in a plausible direction?
- Do liquid level, reflection, flame, material, and gravity behave coherently?
- Are spectacle and sound cues motivated?

One broken physics beat can sink the clip.

## 3. Composition and camera

- Does the frame have a deliberate center of attention?
- Does the event stay readable throughout?
- Is camera movement motivated and restrained?
- Does the aspect ratio strengthen the composition?
- Is there unwanted reframing, zoom, duplicate geometry, or subject drift?

## 4. Continuity

- Same set and object geometry throughout?
- Continuous real time?
- No frozen or repeated frames, stutter, teleportation, reset, unexplained cut, or late scene switch?

## 5. Material and light

- Does light reveal the medium or surface rather than merely decorate it?
- Does the material transformation have a visible before and after?
- Is density restrained enough to stay plausible?

## 6. Audio

- Does the sound palette fit the visible materials?
- Are effects motivated by visible contact or action?
- Are the requested layers present?
- Is any pre-lap, missing foley, or decoupled transient acceptable for the intended edit?

Generated audio adds texture. Production-critical timing and finishing belong in post.

## Verdicts

- **Ship** — compelling, useful, and physically coherent. Record any tolerated defect.
- **Usable with a defect** — strong enough for an edit, with a named limitation or post fix.
- **Experimental** — teaches a capability boundary but is not production-safe.
- **Reject** — physics, continuity, concept, or utility fails.

Judge against these criteria rather than against a preference for the clip you just made. Confirm a shot with whoever owns the edit before treating it as final.
