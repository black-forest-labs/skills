# Concept field guide

Which concepts tend to work as a standalone FLUX 3 text-to-video shot, and which ones are risky. Use this before settling on an idea: reframing a concept is cheaper than repeatedly rerunning a poor fit.

## Reliable patterns

### Atmospheric light in a medium

Use one motivated light event to reveal fog, rain, steam, condensation, glass, or darkness.

Patterns that work:

- a lighthouse beam sweeping sea fog and igniting tide-pool reflections;
- lightning through a wet glasshouse;
- a road flare flooding fog volumetrically;
- sequential practical lights waking toward camera and creating puddle reflections.

Prompt levers:

- show the source;
- name the medium or surface that catches the light;
- use one sweep, strike, flare, or practical-light transition;
- add a same-set, no-transition continuity contract.

### Wet surfaces and reflections

Reflections work best as active physical participants rather than generic gloss.

Patterns that work:

- flood water whose reflection shatters into ribbons and reassembles;
- rainy crosswalk puddles warping headlight reflections, then settling;
- a window reflection tracking a subject's lean.

Prompt levers:

- name what disturbs the reflection;
- describe the deformation and the settling behavior;
- keep camera and reflecting surface legible in one composition.

### Viscous and liquid macro

One material transformation plus one clean payoff is a strong insert structure.

Patterns that work:

- honey coiling on open slate, thinning, snapping, and recoiling;
- espresso crema folding concentrically after an aligned pour;
- a caramel ribbon cooling and combing into glass-fine threads;
- butter sliding on its melt layer, foaming, and browning at the edge.

Prompt levers:

- one material;
- beat-by-beat honest physics;
- hard side or back light that reveals texture;
- one transformation, not a montage.

### Constrained vessel filling

Conditional rather than universally reliable. It holds up with this geometry:

- spout already centered above a wide visible opening;
- short vertical path;
- visible level rise;
- clean stop;
- a final drop, ring, or surface wave.

Unaligned pours and narrow or occluded openings fail.

### Tactile contact and simple ignition

Contact physics works when source, contact point, propagation, and payoff stay in the same close frame.

Patterns that work:

- a gas flame crown racing around a ring in one direction;
- a road flare in fog;
- a matchbook paper tear, gritty strike, and flame hiss as an audio palette.

Boundary: good sound does not mean synchronized sound. Ignition audio can pre-lap or miss the visible moment. Treat generated audio as texture.

### Noir and restrained environments

Near-monochrome, rain, fog, darkness, practical lights, and deliberate compositions are useful starting points. Add one motivated event so the result is not merely attractive but usable.

## Experimental lanes

Use these as tests with explicit review, not as promised production assets:

- fine material behavior — ice skitter, egg crisping, quench bubbles, ferrofluid;
- restrained swarms — motion is plausible but rarely exceptional;
- simple physical text — one handwritten or inked phrase can work; sequential character creation does not;
- product material response — the physics may work while the concept stays boring;
- long single takes — simple events can still stutter or jump scenes.

## Park or reroute

- close human performance;
- action-at-a-distance force transfer or tension;
- CJK text as featured legible copy;
- graphic sans-serif overlays and late-shot text;
- sequential typewriter or stamp mechanics;
- exact audiovisual synchronization or complete foley;
- identity-critical products without conditioning;
- long multi-step narratives with exact timing;
- arbitrary sparks, bells, alarms, or effects with no visible motivation.

## Concept test

Before generating, answer yes to all six:

1. Can the clip be described as one standalone insert?
2. Is there one visible cause, response, and payoff?
3. Are cause and effect close enough to read in one frame?
4. Does light, material, weather, or reflection make the event cinematic?
5. Is the shot interesting even without exact text or sync?
6. Would an editor know where to use it?

If any answer is no, reframe the concept or route it elsewhere.
